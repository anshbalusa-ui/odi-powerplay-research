# What Makes a Successful ODI Powerplay?

## Introduction
Winning in men's One Day International cricket may depend on how teams balance early scoring with wicket preservation. We estimated how first-10-over batting profiles were associated with match winning probability and prepared a prespecified pitch-effect interaction analysis.

## Methods
We analyzed 942 clean men's ODI matches (1,884 team-innings) from 2015–2024. Powerplay runs, wickets, boundary percentage, and dot-ball percentage were extracted from legal deliveries. Fixed logistic models were evaluated chronologically in 71 held-out 2024 matches (142 innings), with match-clustered 95% intervals. Pre-match opposition strength, innings order, toss, venue, year, and competition type were prespecified context variables. Source-stated pre-match pitch effects were reserved for interaction analyses; those results were not reported pending independent double-coding, reconciliation, and sparsity review. 2025+ outcomes were withheld.

## Results
The fixed runs-and-wickets benchmark had validation ROC-AUC 0.740 (95% interval 0.642–0.822). Adding context yielded ROC-AUC 0.708, while the prespecified interaction model yielded 0.710. In model-standardized scenarios, moving from 38 to 57 powerplay runs at two wickets corresponded to a +0.082 predicted-probability contrast; moving from one to two wickets at 47 runs corresponded to a −0.126 contrast. These are observational associations, not causal effects.

## Conclusion
Across this unlocked cohort, early scoring and wicket preservation were associated with subsequent winning probability. The results support an evidence-calibrated framing of powerplay balance rather than a deterministic coaching rule. Pitch-effect claims remain blocked until reliability and reconciliation are complete.
