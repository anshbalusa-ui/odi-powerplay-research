# ODI powerplay trade-off: prior art and novelty boundary

**Scope.** Targeted, adversarial scan of directly relevant ODI/limited-overs studies and adjacent valuation/prediction methods, checked against publisher/author-hosted records or full text where available. This is not an exhaustive systematic review; search coverage and accessible abstracts/full texts are incomplete. “First ten overs” below means the mandatory ODI opening powerplay, not later discretionary powerplays in historical rules.

## What can—and cannot—be claimed

**Defensible contribution wording (subject to the final implemented analysis):** “This study estimates how opening-powerplay runs and wickets lost are conditionally associated with batting-team ODI win probability, alongside pre-match team strength and innings/match context, and tests prespecified innings-order modification. It adds a contemporary, full-cohort observational win-outcome framing to prior powerplay-specific work on historical rule effects and descriptive innings totals.” If the planned temporal design is executed, state its actual development/2024 validation/locked-period boundaries and match-level grouping; do not imply that temporal validation itself is unprecedented.

**Controller wording:** “Controller” may describe only a proposed *decision-support interpretation* of fitted, state-conditional win-probability estimates (or a clearly labeled model-based comparison of candidate choices, if one is actually estimated). The current observational association design does not estimate causal action effects, identify an optimal policy, or implement a reinforcement-learning/dynamic-programming controller. A fitted marginal association surface is not an action policy. Do not say the controller recommends an aggressive/conservative action, optimizes winning, or establishes the value of a wicket in runs unless a separate action/transition model, policy objective, and validation substantiate that claim.

**Prohibited novelty language:** “first-ever” / “first study” of powerplay strategy, wicket-run trade-offs, or ODI win probability; “first to show wickets matter”; a fixed universal “runs per wicket” valuation; causal “effect of aggression” or “optimal strategy” claims from observed associations; and claims of novel temporal validation without a documented search and design-specific qualification. Historical 2015–2024 preliminary modeling has already occurred, so this is not fully preregistered.

**Search boundary.** The table is a deliberately selective map of close overlaps, not evidence that no additional work exists. References were cross-checked through the linked full text/publisher records when accessible; blocked publisher pages and unreported cohort details are identified rather than guessed. Search included ODI first-ten-overs powerplay, run/wicket valuation, match win probability, dynamic programming/strategy, team strength, and validation terms.
